# Second-Order Chemometrics with EEM Fluorescence in R: Olive Oil Adulteration as an Educational Case Study

Data, R scripts, and a tutorial for an undergraduate activity on second-order chemometric algorithms (PARAFAC, Tucker3, and N-PLS) using excitation-emission matrix (EEM) fluorescence spectroscopy.

## About the project

This repository accompanies the manuscript *"Teaching Second-Order Chemometric Algorithms through Excitation-Emission Fluorescence and R: A Chemistry Education Study of Undergraduate Students' Conceptual Understanding, Technical Competence, and Confidence"* (manuscript in preparation; citation details will be added upon publication).

The manuscript is a chemistry education study. It examines how an instructional sequence built on student-generated EEM data and ready-to-use R scripts supports undergraduate students' conceptual understanding, technical competence, and confidence with second-order methods. The adulteration of extra virgin olive oil (EVOO) with soybean oil is used only as the instructional context.

**Scope note:** the models provided here are teaching material. They are valid only for the controlled experimental conditions of this activity and are not a validated method for authenticating EVOO.

## Dataset

The dataset contains **96 EEM spectra** of EVOO samples from **four commercial brands** (coded A, B, C, and D), acquired on a Shimadzu RF-5301PC spectrofluorometer with 13 excitation wavelengths (310-430 nm in 10 nm steps).

| Group | Design | Samples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Adulterated | Soybean oil in EVOO at 14 levels per brand: 0.5, 1.0, 3.0, 5.0, and 7.0% (w/w), then 10.0-90.0% in 10.0% steps | 56 |
| Pure | 10 aliquots of pure EVOO per brand | 40 |

**Sample naming**

* Adulterated samples: brand letter followed by the soybean oil concentration multiplied by 10 and zero-padded to four digits. Example: `B0900` is brand B at 90.0%; `D0010` is brand D at 1.0%; `D0005` is brand D at 0.5%.
* Pure samples: `P` + brand letter + replicate number. Example: `PB02` is replicate 2 of pure brand B.

During the classroom activity, each of the 10 student groups prepared and measured three samples (3.0, 10.0, and 90.0% soybean oil) of one brand. These spectra were merged with the dataset previously generated by the researchers, and the complete 96-sample dataset was used for modeling.

## Software

* R (developed with version 4.3.1) and RStudio. RStudio is strongly recommended because the scripts use `rstudioapi` dialogs to select files and folders.
* The scripts check for the packages they need and install missing ones automatically. Packages used across the scripts: `multiway`, `ThreeWay`, `sNPLS`, `R.matlab`, `prospectr`, `MASS`, `caret`, `Metrics`, `plotly`, `plot3D`, `abind`, `parallel`, `rstudioapi`, `dplyr`, `zoo`, `signal`, and `baseline`.

## Workflow summary

1. **Data preparation** (scripts [1] to [3]): import raw files, build the 3D array, pre-process.
2. **Second-order modeling** (scripts [4] to [7]): decomposition or N-PLS, outlier diagnosis (Hotelling's T² vs. Q residuals), train/test partitioning (70%/30%), model building, and validation.
   * Train/test partitioning uses Kennard-Stone in the LDA scripts ([4], [5]) and the MLM approach (Kennard-Stone followed by a mutation step) in the regression scripts ([6], [7]).
3. **Evaluation:** accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity for classification; R², RMSE, and MAE for regression.

The scripts are distributed with baseline parameters (for example, 7 factors in [4], Tucker3 ranks of [7, 7, 7] in [5], 8 factors in [6], and 9 latent variables in [7]) and with example lists of samples to remove as outliers. These are starting points for the classroom activity, not optimized values; users are expected to review and adjust them (see `Tutorial.md`).

## Educational application

The activity was implemented in the theoretical/practical Chemometrics course (QUI0610) at UFRN in the second semester of 2025, with approximately 40 undergraduate chemistry students over eight 50-minute sessions: a theoretical foundation, a wet-lab phase (sample preparation and EEM acquisition), and a dry-lab phase (chemometric modeling in R). The learning outcomes, assessment instruments, and their limitations are reported in the manuscript and its Supporting Information.

## How to use

1. Install R and RStudio.
2. Clone or download this repository.
3. To reproduce the modeling only, open any script from [4] to [7] in RStudio and select `Samples/eem_cube_cleaned.mat` when prompted.
4. To reproduce the pre-processing from the raw files, extract `Samples/Raw files.rar`, then run scripts [1], [3], and the modeling scripts in order.
5. Read `Tutorial.md` for details on parameters, outlier removal, and troubleshooting.
